## What the sources say

- **Scope and AI:** §1.A covers API-supplied content/information and reserves rights concerning derivatives and AI/ML use. §1.C separately prohibits derivatives. Its final restriction is exactly: “Use the TMDB APIs or TMDB Content in connection with, including for training, a machine learning (ML) or artificial intelligence (AI) based Application.” The wording includes API use and connection with an application; it is not expressed solely as a model-training restriction. [API terms, §§1.A–C](https://www.themoviedb.org/api-terms-of-use)
- **Caching and termination:** §1.C prohibits caching any information obtained from TMDB/API for longer than six months. §1.D requires stopping API/content/key use and promptly purging all TMDB content, including cached content, on termination. The text does not prescribe a field-specific refresh algorithm or explicitly resolve renewed fetches, unchanged records, IDs, embeddings, backups, logs, exports, or deletion deadlines beyond those requirements. [API terms, §§1.C–D](https://www.themoviedb.org/api-terms-of-use)
- **Commercial use:** §2 requires a separate written agreement and includes direct/indirect revenue, specified destination/search/query-response uses (expressly including LLMs/chatbots), and training/validation or supplying datasets for those purposes among its examples. TMDB retains discretion over classification. A commercial agreement must expressly permit the intended use; this report has no such agreement to examine. [API terms, §2](https://www.themoviedb.org/api-terms-of-use)
- **FAQ qualification:** the FAQ describes attribution-based free noncommercial access, revenue creation as the primary-purpose test in its summary, and contacting TMDB for commercial licensing. That summary supplies no AI exemption and does not establish this product's classification under the fuller terms. [FAQ, commercial service, commercial/developer API, API-key cost](https://developer.themoviedb.org/docs/faq)
- **Attribution:** §3 requires the TMDB logo, lower prominence than the application's identity, no implied endorsement, and a prominent notice covering API use and lack of endorsement/certification/approval. The FAQ additionally specifies approved logos and an About/Credits location; its sample notice differs from the terms' sample. Record the applicable notice and branding requirements during permission verification rather than treating either abbreviated wording as a reviewed release requirement. [API terms, §3](https://www.themoviedb.org/api-terms-of-use), [FAQ, attribution and branding](https://developer.themoviedb.org/docs/faq)
- **Other obligations:** §1.B incorporates the general site terms; §10.H allows posted modifications. This focused check does not review every incorporated provision, rights in third-party images, an account's accepted agreement, or a negotiated license. The FAQ itself disclaims ownership of API images/data. [API terms, §§1.B and 10.H](https://www.themoviedb.org/api-terms-of-use), [FAQ, legal notice](https://developer.themoviedb.org/docs/faq)

## Historical workflow questions, superseded by the confirmation above

| Proposed flow | Verified distinction and unanswered applicability |
| --- | --- |
| Extract titles from the user's own notes/photos using the LLM Gateway, then perform ordinary catalog search | TMDB documents search followed by ID-based detail lookup. User input extraction can be described separately from supplying TMDB data to a model. **Inference:** that separation makes the question precise but does not establish an exemption from §1.C's application-level wording. Ask whether an AI-assisted importer may use the API after extraction even when no TMDB fields enter the LLM. Also distinguish handwritten notes from screenshots containing provider descriptions/images. [Search workflow](https://developer.themoviedb.org/docs/search-and-query-for-details), [terms, §1.C](https://www.themoviedb.org/api-terms-of-use) |
| Store reusable catalog copies and refresh them | The six-month cache limit is explicit; perpetual storage rights and timer-reset semantics are not established. TMDB documents changed-ID lists and per-item field changes, a technical synchronization mechanism rather than a license extension. Ask what counts as caching/refresh, what must expire, and whether IDs, historical versions, replicas and backups receive different treatment. [Terms, §§1.C–D](https://www.themoviedb.org/api-terms-of-use), [Tracking Changes](https://developer.themoviedb.org/docs/tracking-content-changes) |
| Embed TMDB overviews for semantic retrieval without training/fine-tuning a model | Absence of training does not answer the broader AI/ML and derivative restrictions. No reviewed source expressly permits embedding generation or retention, vector indexes, retrieval-augmented generation, or supplying text to an external embedding service. Ask for explicit coverage of generation, model/gateway handling, vector storage, search, refresh and deletion. [Terms, §§1.A–C and 2](https://www.themoviedb.org/api-terms-of-use) |
| Send followed people and seen movies to an LLM for suggestions | A user's Follow/seen choices have different provenance from provider IDs, names, titles and descriptions. Provider payloads still raise the AI restriction even if inference-only or no-training settings are used. The sources provide no explicit IDs-only or short-title exception. Ask separately about IDs, names/titles, overviews, generated outputs, prompt logging and gateway retention; user permission and model-vendor settings do not by themselves demonstrate TMDB permission. [Terms, §§1.A–C and 2](https://www.themoviedb.org/api-terms-of-use) |

**Architectural inference:** keeping app-owned Movie/Person identities, Follow/Watchlist data, provider provenance and refreshable metadata separate would make any eventual retention/purge obligation easier to implement. Derived indexes, model requests, telemetry, exports and backup recovery need the same provenance discussion. This is a boundary proposal conditional on the eventual permitted uses, not a conclusion that those uses are permitted.
